# Claim Safety

Use this reference before any rewrite.

## Non-Negotiable Rules

- Keep completed, partial, planned, and speculative work distinct.
- Preserve all quantitative values unless the user asks for a correction or an obvious formatting fix.
- Do not turn an implementation detail into a scientific claim.
- Do not turn a visualization trend into statistical significance.
- Do not invent citations, priority statements, reviewer comparisons, or baselines.
- If a result is based on a subset of experiments, state the subset.

## Match each claim to its evidence

Before rewriting a core claim, identify what supports it, under which conditions, and what does not follow. This is an editing decision, not a table for the author. A proof holds on its stated domain; an instrument reading is a measurement; a simulation is a numerical observation, not a measurement. A proposed mechanism may explain an observation without being established by it; a hypothesis is not a finding. Choose the accurate verb, not the weakest one.

In results prose, lead with the finding, give its evidence and put the necessary scope where the reader first needs it (in the opening clause if the finding would otherwise be false). This is not a sentence order for methods or proofs. A negative finding can itself be the result: no collisions among the tested inputs does not establish injectivity.

Ask of each qualifier: **Would removing or moving it change where the claim holds, the kind of evidence, or what the reader can infer?**

- **Delete redundancy** if scope and uncertainty are unchanged. Read `may`, `potentially` and `suggest` against the proposition each modifies; do not search-and-replace them.
- **State the tested scope positively** when it is known: identify the conditions of the observation rather than listing every untested condition.
- **Retain** a premise, necessary negation, statistical uncertainty, counterexample or unverified explanation that changes the reading. An association does not establish cause; a numerical difference alone does not establish statistical significance.
- **Merge or move** only when the same claim, population, batch and conditions remain unambiguous at each use. Read the abstract and any standalone caption on their own before removing a repeated condition.
- **Keep an open question outside the prose** in the existing author notes when it is a conjectured objection, missing material to check or optional follow-up. If the claim itself lacks support, propose a narrower claim or leave it pending; do not merely remove the warning. For locked or approved text, flag the change for the author rather than making it.

## Revision Notes

If a rewrite softens or narrows a claim, state that in `Revision notes:`. If a claim cannot be fixed without new data, put it under `Needs author check:`.
